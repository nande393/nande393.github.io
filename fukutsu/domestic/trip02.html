Horyu-ji(temple) / Asuka-mura(village) / Kumano-Kodo(old path)

This is the first trip using my car for more than one night. My wife made this trip plan that main destination were Horyu-ji, Asuka-mura and Kumano-Kodo, and stopped at some fascinated places next to next on the route.
In this trip of eight days and seven nights we drove 2,168 kilometers. although, when we arrived home, we were exhausted, we enjoyed our handmade trip.

Izumo-taisha(big shrine) / Tottori Sand Dunes / Amanohashidate / Sekigahara historic battlefield

Isumo-taishaTottori-sakyuAmanohashidateSekigahara

Izumo-taisha is one of the most ancient and important Shinto shrines in Japan. No record gives the date of establishment.
There are myths around this shrine and this area. In fact the myths show us the importance for Japanese history.
I saw Tottori Sand Dunes for thet fist time. It was large unexpectedly.
Amanohashidate is one of the three most beautiful views in Japan. When a person looked at it upside down, it looked like a bridge built over the sky. That is why this place is so called.
This place, Sekigahara, is no more than fields and mountains. But this name is very famous in Japan. In 1600 the historic battle did here and it was turning point for Tokugawa Ieyasu to take his government.

Horyu-ji

Yumedonofive-story pagodaNishiin-garanKondo

Ho-ryu-ji is a Buddhist temple in Ikaruga, Nara Prefecture. The temple's pagoda is widely acknowledged to be of the oldest wooden buildings existing in the world. In 1993 Ho-ryu-ji was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The temple was originally commissioned by Prince Shotoku. This first temple is believed to have been completed by 607.
Ho-ryu-ji was a bueatiful temple, and I was impressed and surprised at the solemnity and the historic structures.

Kumano-kodo

Kiiji A1Kiiji A2Hongu TaishaNachi TaishaKiiji B1Kiiji B2

The kumano-kodo are pilgrimage routes. There are some routes to Kumano from some big towns. One route of them called Kiiji is from Osaka to Kumano. We took two parts of this route, and both parts are close to Kumano.
We stopped at Kumano-hongu-taisha to park our car. Next we took a cab to Fushiogami-oji, then we walked on Kumano-kodo A, 4 kilomaters, to Kumano-hongu-taisha.
Furthermore we went to Daimon-saka and kept our car in a parking. Getting on a bus, we arrived at Nachi-taisha. After looking around famous Nachi water-fall, we strolled on Kumano-kodo B, 2.7 kilometers, and went back to Daimon-saka parking lot.

Korakuen is one of the three great gardens of Japan. It was built in 1700 by Ikeda Tsunamasa, lord of Okayama and it is one of the daimyo gardens representing the Edo era.
