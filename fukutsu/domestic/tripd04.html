Todaiji(Nara) and Osaka


I've been to Osaka several times, but I didn't know much about what it was actually like. This time, following Kyoto last year, I decided to visit famous tourist spots in Osaka with my wife. We will also stop by Todaiji Temple. Although I visited this area, I missed seeing the Great Buddha because it was past its closing time.
I thought that Osaka is a city that is popular for its popular culture and common people's food, so I determined to go there to enjoy it.

First day

Todaiji temple in Narathe Great Buddha of NaraHeijo Palace ruinsHeijo Palace ruins

I took the Shinkansen to Shin-Osaka and then immediately took the train to Nara. The round bus called 'Gruto bus' was convenient for visiting tourist spots, so I used it to get to Todaiji Temple. Both the temple and Nara Daibutsu were large enough, but Nara Daibutsu was smaller than I expected. I saw many worshipers and many deer playing.
After that, we took the same bus to the Heijo Palace ruins. I was very interested in the old capital. What kind of lifestyle did people and live at that time?
Heijo Palace ruins
'The capital Heijo-kyo was built at the site of present-day Nara city about 1,300 years ago. A series of laws known as the Ritsuryo codes had been completed to provide a legal framework for the nation, and Tempyo culture flourished in and around the city. For the 74 years of what is known as the Nara period, the city stood at the center of Japanese political and cultural life. In the center of Heijo-kyo were located the Daigokuden (Imperial Audience Hall) and Chodo-in, which were used for political and ceremonial purposes, as well as the emperorfs palace and the government agencies that governed the city.'

Second day

Universal Studios Japan 1USJ 2USJ 3USJ 4

I hurried to Universal Studios Japan before it opened. When we arrived there, there had been already a long line of people.
First, we went to the Harry Potter area. I didn't know much about other regions and wasn't very interested. So I walked around the town that looked like the movie set. Lining very long, we approached the school where Harry studied, then we toured the school's cinematic interior. Furthermore, we were led into a line by many people. We didn't know what would happen there next. Anyway, after waiting in line, I took my seat on the ride and it was a different world. As soon as the seats moved we followed Harry, moving up and down and left and right, just like we were playing a ball game in the movies. A bit fearing, we were very surprised and impressed. It made us a lot of fun even for old people of us. Then we browed ather areas a bit and strolled streets. We were really exsaused. Before lunch we left USJ because I felt sick to my intesting.

Third day

Osaka CasleJanjan side streetShinsekaiTsutenkaku tower


Early in the morning, we took the train to Osaka Castle, walked around Osaka Castle Park, and entered the Osaka Castle Museum as the first private customers. After admiring the cityscape, we boarded 'Gozabune boat' and admired the castle from the moat that surrounds it. I arrived at the JR station using a bus that looked like a train. We headed to Tsutenkaku. Surrounding the tower is a downtown area called "Shinsekai." We entered Janjan Yokomichi and had lunch at a restaurant eating the famous ``kushikatsu''. As the reviews said, it was delicious and reasonably priced. Then, I wanted to take part in the Donbori Cruise, so I headed to Dotonbori via the Shinsekai market. Because the weather forecast said it would rain the next day. It was a very fun cruise because the guide's lady was a funny Osaka girl.
On the way of the hotel we rode 'Hep Five Ferris Wheel'. And I found around Osaka station compricated, seeing the ground from up site.

Forth day

DotonboriDonbori CruiseUmeda sky building America villege

In the morning, I walked to Umeda Sky Building for about 30 minutes and went up to the observation deck.
After that, we took the train to "America Village". There was nothing special about it, but I ate 'Takoyaki' there.
Osaka Metro took us to Dotonbori, the center of Osaka. We strolled around the arcade and along the river. Hozenji Yokocho is a special place with a nostalgic atmosphere.
Then, we visited Dotonbori ZAZA, where some manzai groups performed for 30 minutes. The audience was small, about 10 people, so the performers spoke to us directly and privately. It was fun and interesting.

Fifth day

Dotonbori Hozenji side streetDotonbori ZAZA Nakanoshima ParkNishinari

On the fifth day, I headed to Nakanoshima Park, which I somehow remembered since I went there at the end of high school. That's why the park is a special place for me. Then, I went to Nishinari, which was once considered a dangerous area, and still has a bit of atmosphere and cheap food. I went to a cheap izakaya and ate "Hormone" while drinking. I arrived at the Shinkansen station early. So, at the station, I changed my reservation to a train 3 hours earlier.

Afterword
The hotel was not far away, but it was difficult to find the way to the hotel. I used Google Maps on my phone, but as for me it was difficult to use. I asked many people ways and I found them very kind and frendly. And even some people said to us 'May I help you?'
Osaka e-Pass ( 2 Day's Osaka Rakuyu Pass ) I reserved on the internet was convinience. The pass that we bought for ¥ 2,500 was worth a total of ¥ 6,200 for Gozabune boat, Tsutenkaku, Donbori Cruise, the Hep Five Ferris Wheel, Umeda Sky Building Observation and Dotonbori ZAZA.
This time, I felt smartphone was very importnt tool to make a trip. I made reservation before trip for USJ, Osaka Castle, Osaka Pass and used them, showing their QR cord on my phone.
The tourist spots and downtown areas were crowded with people, and there seemed to be a lot of foreigners, so we had to wait in long lines every time we wanted to do something.
We really enjoyed this trip because we fell in love with Osaka's culture and food.
