Yokohama Chinatown and Tokyo


This time, I wanted to visit three groups of places with my wife. First, Yokohama Chinatown; second, trendy new buildings in Tokyo; and third, some remaining places in Tokyo that we hadn't visited yet.
By the way, this trip was in danger because of Typhoon 10. However, on September 1st, it weakened off the coast of Tokaido and became a tropical depression. We flew in strong winds. When we were landing, it was raining.

First day

Chinatown Harbor View ParkYamashita ParkRed Brick Warehouse

Early in the morning, we left home and took a flight to Haneda. As soon as we arrived in Yokohama around noon, we went to Chinatown after checking into the hotel. Although it was sometimes raining in Chinatown, we walked the streets, bought dim sum at some shops and ate them.
After we left Chinatown, we visited popular tourist spots: Yokohama Harbor View Park, Yamashita Park, and the Red Brick Warehouse. Although the occasional rain, I didn't find the park faced the sea beautiful because of the narrow harbor and its messy facilities. After sightseeing,
we returned to the hotel. Later, we went to Chinatown again for dinner. It was busier than during the day, with bright signboards lighting up the area. It was past 8 o'clock when we got back to the hotel.

Second day

Port Opening Memorial Hall Kanagawa Prefectural OfficeYokohama CustomsUeno Onshi Park

In the early hours, we went to Chinatown to eat rice porridge. On the way, we met an old woman who lived in Chinatown and was taking a walk. She asked us how we liked the Chinese food. I said I was disappointed because of the terrible taste. She asked which restaurant we went to yesterday and said, "If I had met you before you entered the restaurant, I could have told you about a nice, delicious restaurant."
We dropped by the hotel, then checked out and started sightseeing. We visited Yokohama's three towers: the Port Opening Memorial Hall, Kanagawa Prefectural Office, and Yokohama Customs, which were built in the Taisho and early Showa eras. They are popular as historical and fascinating buildings that match the coast.
Then we headed to Ueno in Tokyo from Yokohama by JR. Arriving at Ueno, we looked around Ueno Park. Of course, I saw the Saigo Takamori Statue. Shinobazu Pond and Bentendo were impressive. Leaving the park behind, we strolled through Ameyoko, which has a lot of various kinds of stalls, fish sellers, drink and eat shops, and daily use shops. We had a late lunch at a famous meat shop restaurant and found it tasted good. Exhausted, we arrived at the hotel.

Third day

Tsukiji outer marketNihonbashiHama-rikyu GardensAmeyoko


At six o'clock, we left the hotel for Tsukiji Outer Market by subway. It was bustling even early in the morning, with a lot of people gathering. We ate special rice balls and dashimaki tamago for breakfast at famous shops.
After strolling around, we went to the Ginza and Nihonbashi areas to revisit those places.
Next, we headed to Hama-rikyu Gardens. "Hama-rikyu Gardens is a representative Edo daimyo garden owned by the Tokugawa Shogunate."
Then we went to Ameyoko again. We arrived at the hotel before 1 o'clock. Two hours later, we went out of the hotel and found that Kappabashi was not far away. So we explored the low city area of Tokyo and then reached Kappabashi, which has an alley of kitchen goods shops. It was about 4:30 PM when we returned to the hotel.

Forth day

Tokyu Kabukicho TowerTokyu Plaza HarajukuAzabudai Hills Odaiba

I wanted to see and experience new buildings in Tokyo because they look like they use cutting-edge technology and new sensibilities. In the morning, I took the JR to Tokyu Kabukicho Tower in Shinjuku, which took about an hour. The new amusement facility was not open yet, but it had a 24-hour food court decorated with lots of lights that looked odd but somewhat sensitive. After that, we took the JR again to Tokyu Plaza Harajuku, which had fantastic design. Then we visited the faithful dog Hachiko and the scramble intersection in Shibuya. Azabudai Hills, which is 330 meters high and now the tallest building in Japan, was big and solemn both outside and inside unlike anything I had ever seen before.
Then we went to Odaiba. There, we visited DiverCity Tokyo Plaza, the Fuji TV Hachitama spherical observation deck, the Statue of Liberty, and Odaiba Seaside Park. Odaiba was an attractive place for shopping, relaxing, and sightseeing.
By the way, it took a lot of time to go to and from Odaiba. We got back to the hotel past 6 ofclock.

Fifth day

The Toyosu ShijoToyosu SenkyakubanraiFukagawa Edo MuseumFukagawa

It was the last day of our trip. We started early and headed to Toyosu Market. The market, built in 2018, was big enough and functional, attracting a lot of people from every region and even foreign countries. It has various facilities for shopping and eating, especially Sennkyaku Banrai, which delights customers and visitors.
After arriving in Fukagawa, we visited the Fukagawa Edo Museum and had lunch with Asari Clam Mixed Rice at a nearby place.
We left Fukagawa for Haneda Airport to go back home. Unfortunately, our flight was more than an hour behind schedule . On the way we have a small supper at Hakata Station and finally, we got home half past 9 o'clock.

Afterword
Although the food in Yokohama Chinatown was disappointing and did not meet my expectations, its prosperity and atmosphere were impressive.

The three new buildings we visited were remarkable and gave me a glimpse of the new age. If I had experienced the virtual theater in Tokyu Kabukicho Tower, I would have felt an even greater sense of modernity.

During this trip, we visited many places: Ameyoko, the scramble intersection in Shibuya, Odaiba, and Toyosu Market, among others. Hama-rikyu Gardens and Ueno Park were also nice.

It was hard work for us as we walked more than 10 km, about 4 hours each day.

We encountered Typhoon 10 during this trip, and I thought our airplane passed through it on the way to Haneda. By the way, perhaps every one was afraid of and careful about it, so the airplane had quite few people. Besides, three days before, our suitcase was not accepted by the carrier to be sent to the hotel. However, the weather was not as hot compared to before the typhoon during the trip.

Using a smartphone for cashless payments has clearly advanced at convenience stores, transportation, lockers in stations, food shops, and facility tickets. At the airport, I was surprised by the web check-in and automatic baggage receiver system.

On the fourth day, an unfortunate incident happened in Shinjuku. I had an abdominal problem and was urgently looking for a toilet, but there were none around. Eventually, I soiled my underwear and pants. After that, I found a toilet near an underground entrance. My wife left me in the toilet to buy new underwear and pants. She asked a young man if he could help her. He was so kind that he went shopping with her and brought the items to me in the toilet. It was an amazingly fortunate thing, and I am very grateful.

This trip had both happy and unhappy moments, making it very memorable. Anyway, we enjoyed Yokohama and Tokyo very much.
