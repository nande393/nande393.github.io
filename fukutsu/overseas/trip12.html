The Philippines

I visited The Philippines for 2 weeks in Cebu and for 3 days in Manila with my wife.
In Cebu I studied English 6 hours a day on weekdays as man-to-man lesson. And on holiday we went sightseeing. In Manila we joined in the city tour daylong and walked around Makati and Ermita areas on other day.
Before coming here, we were worried about the public safety of The Philippines, especially Manila. However we enjoyed sightseeing, Philippine food and studying English.

The path that we always passed through for a shortcut to school was near our hotel. On the way we first pass the path, and go through a church garden, and next go along a high school, then get a main street. It takes 5 minutes. A part beside this path looked like a small slum, so I felt uncomfortable a bit.
The center of Cebu is not so modern and not big but no more than a ordinary small city. The suburb looks dirty and messing a little.
The Philippines is one of developing countries. It has many similarities with others. There are many poor people and some rich people. There are some dirty areas and some modern places in this country.
I really expect that this country is getting better and better.

Island Hopping Tour

Olang Island Caohagan Island Caohagan Island BBQ in Caohagan Island

We took part in a "Island Hopping Tour". Most of these tour are hold personally, so the costs become high. On the internet I found this kind of tour was about 5,000P. I asked our school about the tour, then our school informed us of a tour of 3,700P and tried to gather members to cut price down, finally 8 members joined in the tour of 2,700P.
At first we went to Olang Island and enjoyed snorkelling around the sea of the island. The sea was very beautiful and small tropical fish groups gathered around us and various fish passed us, rocks and coral reef. After snorkelling we went to Caohagan Island which had a white sand beach with some local lodges. We ate BBQ of fish, shells, crabs, pork and chicken with fruit. And we were full of them. We spent wonderful time with the beautiful beach and good food. And we walked around inside the Island and saw the life of local people.
There is a interesting story. This island owner is a Japanese and he made effort to give inhabitants jobs and education, living with local people. He made some lodge and toilets for sightseeing to make jobs, and started quilt making. And he also extended elementary school and founded a scholarship for local child.

Cebu sightseeing

Fort San Pedro Magellan's Cross Santo Nino Church the square of the church

It took about 10 minutes by taxi to go to Santo Nino Church, and around the church there were some tourist spots where we looked and walked around.
Fort San Pedro
"This is the oldest and smallest fort in the Philippines. It was a triangular bastion initially built with logs and mud in 1565. When the Spanish came, it was rebuilt with stones and mortar and believed to be finished on 1738."
Magellan's Cross
"Located next to Sto. Nino Church, is the chapel where the Magellanfs Cross is housed. But, we couldn't see inside the house, because it was under constructon. This wooden cross is a Christian cross planted by Magellan to celebrate the Roman Catholic baptism of Rajah Humabon, his wife Queen Juana, and some of their followers on April 21, 1521."
Santo Nino Church
"Considered as the oldest Roman Catholic church in the Philippines, it was built on the spot where the image of the Santo Nino de Cebu was found in 1565 by Spanish explorers."
We visited other places, The Independence Plaza and City Hall.

Makati in Manila

in front of the hotel Makati shopping mall shopping mall

I was surprised at the hotel in Makati area we stayed, when we arrived. We got checked bodies and luggage in the taxi, in addition they used a dog for drag. We couldn't believe this manners.
Makati area in Manila is a business area and a international city which expels poor people has a lot of tall buildings and huge shopping malls. In Manila it looked efficient for people to work. However Cebu and Manila have a lot of security guards with guns or rifles. Every shop has guards and even a small shop like a convenience store has a guard. Other areas get complicated in the modern and the poor.
I saw many young men gathering in the center of Manila and asked them what they were doing. They answered they were looking for jobs.
I felt big energy that would make this country improve in near future.

Manila sightseeing

American Cemetery San Agustin Church Manila Cathedral Fort Santiago

The most of Cultural World Heritage in Philippines are churches which were built about 400 years ago in Spain era and I felt sad to know the fact.
American Cemetery
"Most of the 17,201 American buried here. The cemetery is peaceful with plenty of shade and lovely views and anybody with an interest in history will enjoy the extensive memorials and maps which give some insight into the war in the region."
San Agustin Church
"Within Intramuros stands Manila's oldest stone church, San Agustin, which was completed in 1607 and has since survived the ravages of time and successive invasions. The church has a magnificent and intricately-carved door, a Baroque pulpit, and an 18th-century pipe organ."
Manila Cathedral
"Manila Cathedral is one of the oldest and most famous churches in Asia. It has the designs and colors of the church's stained glass windows, and the Largest Church-Stationed Pipe Organ in Southeast Asia. "
Fort Santiago
"One of the most important historical sites in Manila, Fort Santiago was built by the Spanish conquistador Miguel Lopez de Legazpi as a defensive fortress designed to protect the newly formed city of Manila. It is a key feature of the famous Walled City of Manila, which is referred to as Intramuros."
Rizal Park
"About 60 hectares Rizal Park is named for Dr Jose Rizal, renowned Philippine anti-colonialist, writer and philosopher, whose execution in 1896 sparked the Philippine Revolution. The park is one of the largest in Southeast Asia,"

Ermita in Manila

a market Bay side Road inside Intramuros outside Intramuros

I stayed in Ermita area for two days. Most people even in Cebu said Manila is dangerous, especially Ermita. We could see many poor people, and beggers holding a baby or bringing child, and touts like stalkers annoyed us. Many people said, "don't get near to a local market where thieves and bad boys will gather." Bay side road is not clean, and small and dirty shops were opened by poor people, but the sea was very picturesque.
"The oldest part of Manila is the medieval Spanish walled enclave on the southern bank of the Pasig River, packed with historic buildings and churches." We walked around Intramuros. Most old historical building were destroyed by wars and became like ruins. In Intramuros some places were under construction and the town were renewing.

Anyway we enjoyed The Philippines a lot.
