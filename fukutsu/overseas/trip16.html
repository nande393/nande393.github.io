New York & Boston, Philadelphia, Washington DC, Niagara Falls

I planned a individual trip to New York for sixteen days. Because I want to look around slowly and relaxedly the city to enjoy more. The eastern part of the United States has many fascinated places, too. Finally, I decided to visit Boston personally and to take part in a Philadelphia & Washington DC tour and a Niagara Falls tour. I would stay at New York for 8 days and join two local tours, 1-day New York City Tour and Night View Tour.
Before the trip, I worried if we could do it, because my English is still weak and I couldn't image the situation or circumstance there. However, I encouraged myself, maybe this is the last trip for aged us as an adventurous and risky trip.
Now we live in convenience world, on the Internet, we were able to book airline tickets, hotels and local tours, and were able to reserve even tickets of Bus, Amtrak and United Nations Guided Tours, before the trip.

M.I.T.Harvard UniversityBoston CommonQuincy Market

At 6:00 we left the hotel for Boston. We took E line at "Queens Plaza subway station" to Manhattan.. I got on a train and felt an old and bad image of U.S. subway. As soon as we arrived in Boston, we headed to "Massachusetts Institute of Technology" by subway. Then we went up the entrance but we didn't understand where we were. I asked a young woman "Where is MIT? ". She said "Around here is MIT" and "Where do you want to go?" I answered we were looking for a student dining hall. Then she showed the way, writing the map on a piece of paper. We strolled MIT buildings and found the hall. We ate big size lunch there. A side of MIT is the river with a public walk. Some of building are unique and artistic. Everything is clean and beautiful.
Then we left MIT for Harvard University. It is also divided into many parts of buildings, but it has the broad campus. It is calm and in authority. Finally, we called on the "coop".
Both school make up Town itself.

The information office of Boston Common is the start point for Freedom Trail. The red line in front of the office leads Freedom Trail. Walking on this line, we looked and felt the old calm town.
"The Freedom Trail is a 4- km-long path through downtown Boston, that passes by 16 locations significant to the history of the United States. Marked largely with brick, it winds between Boston Common to the Bunker Hill Monument. Stops along the trail include simple explanatory ground markers, graveyards, notable churches and buildings, and a historic naval frigate."
We stopped the trail at Quincy Market, and strolled, then we took away famous baked lobster roll and cream chowder. Later, we ate it at the hotel.

Next day we went to the Bunker Hill, the ending point, and continue the way to the inverse direction At Quincy Market we ate cream chowder bread & lobster salad roll for lunch, too.
Then we used Amtrak said like "Shinkansen". Scenery and time was almost the same as a bus but a comfortable seat.
